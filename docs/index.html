Built around how people here eat

Star ratings won't tell you which town claims the tenderloin or which drive-in still pours its own root beer. Indiana Eats is organized the way people here eat: breaded tenderloins, drive-ins and root beer, Hoosier pie and Amish-country tables, each place checked in fall 2026 against a 2025–26 source or an Indiana tourism office's own page. Apple Maps' live card supplies ratings, hours and photos.

Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out between towns.

What stays on your phone

Indiana Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is Indiana Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Two kinds of source. The breaded tenderloin, drive-in, Hoosier pie, Amish country and oldest-places lists are our own research from fall 2026, with a dated 2025 or 2026 source or an Indiana tourism office's own page behind each place. The wider directory of 13,543 restaurants starts from open map listings (Overture Maps), filtered to the kinds that proved reliable where states publish restaurant lists.

Where can I get a breaded tenderloin?

Our tenderloin list has 4 hand-checked places, from Nick's Kitchen in Huntington, which claims the sandwich, to Indianapolis drive-ins and Kokomo's King Tenderloin. In the web search, the list puts the nearest first once you share your location.

How do I check an Indiana restaurant's inspection?

Indiana's 95 local health departments inspect restaurants and publish their own reports, through a search page, a weekly or monthly report, or on request. None posts a grade. Vanderburgh and Porter counties publish weekly and monthly reports, so places matched to them show each inspection's date and the county's count of violations, as published; the app computes no grade. Every place links the state's list of local health departments.

Does it show which places serve alcohol?

No. The Indiana Alcohol and Tobacco Commission permits the bars and restaurants that pour alcohol, but its permits sit behind a search form we don't use, so the app doesn't say either way.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
